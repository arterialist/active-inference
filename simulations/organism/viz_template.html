A life, not a loop

The same three-region insect brain — mushroom body (learns odour value), central complex (path-integrates home), action selection (a 4-way winner-take-all) — but run over a long horizon in a harder world: seven food sources (some nutritious, some toxic) and a predator that actively patrols and gives chase. All four behaviours emerge from the interplay of the brain and the world, none are scripted: it forages when it smells food and is hungry, flees the predator, path-integrates home with a full crop, and explores when fed or when nothing is nearby — and once it has been poisoned, it learns to steer away from the toxic smell.

What "complex" does — and doesn't — mean here

What is real: the behaviour is genuinely context-dependent and shaped by experience, not a fixed script. Which of the four actions wins is decided every tick by a spiking winner-take-all reading four internal drives (hunger, fear, crop-full, satiety); the approach-vs-avoid decision comes from a mushroom body that learned the odour→value association from being poisoned; the way home is a path integral accumulated in the central complex. Change the world and the life changes with it.
What is bounded: this is action-selection, not planning. The organism has no world-model and no goals beyond its drives, so it cannot form multi-step plans, and over very long excursions the path-integrated home vector accumulates drift — if the predator harasses it far enough off course, it will abandon the trip and resume foraging rather than find the nest. The richness you see is emergent from a handful of drives meeting a demanding environment; it is real ethology, but it is not open-ended cognition.
